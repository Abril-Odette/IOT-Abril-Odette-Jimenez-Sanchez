MY FAVORITES

The Strokes have long been one of my absolute favorite bands, largely due to their signature sound, effortless personality, and the captivating dramaticism built into their music.
Their influence on modern rock is undeniable—so much so that Alex Turner, lead singer of another favorite band of mine, Arctic Monkeys, famously opened their 2018 album Tranquility Base Hotel & Casino with the iconic line: 'I just wanted to be one of The Strokes.'


Below are three of my favorite songs by the band, each holding a special place in my playlist.


Eternal Summer

Why I Chose It:

It is a dramatic, multi-layered track with an explosive vocal performance from Julian Casablancas, seamlessly shifting between smooth psychedelic pop and aggressive post-punk outbursts.

Background & Meaning:

The song tackles the urgent reality of climate change and societal denial. The title ironically contrasts the idea of a pleasant summer with the grim reality of global warming and rising temperatures.

Fun Fact:

During the chorus, Casablancas delivers raw, scream-like vocals reminiscent of 1980s punk rock, contrasting sharply with the upbeat, disco-infused bassline running through the verses.

Ode to the Mets

Why I Chose It:

It serves as a deeply atmospheric, melancholic masterpiece that builds up layer by layer, creating a profound emotional climax.

Background & Meaning:

The song revolves around nostalgia, regret, passage of time, and accepting defeat. It uses the New York Mets baseball team as a metaphor for lingering hope despite recurring disappointment.

Fun Fact:

The studio recording opens with Casablancas saying, "Yeah, drums please, Fab," a spontaneous cue to drummer Fabrizio Moretti that was kept in the final mix and quickly became a fan-favorite meme.

Background & Meaning:

The lyrical content critiques modern civilization, consumerism, human apathy, and the commercialization of society (referencing suffixes like "-ize" in words like materialize, fertilize, sanitize).

Fun Fact:

The song cuts off abruptly mid-word on the line "fury to be fully / furi-..." on the final syllable of "furious" (or "fury-ized"). This intentional cut symbolizes sudden extinction or complete destruction without warning.
